Cons of Computer Science for Environmental Sustainability

While computer science has the potential to drive sustainable solutions, it also comes with significant challenges. From energy-intensive infrastructures to widening social divides, it's essential to critically assess these drawbacks.

High Energy Consumption of Data Centers

Data centers powering cloud computing and AI require enormous energy, frequently relying on non-renewable sources. This contributes to increased carbon emissions if energy is not offset with sustainable methods.

It is difficult to find accurate data regarding energy consumption of generative AI due to companies not releasing it to the public. Some estimates suggest that an AI generation could consume 10 times more electricity than a Google search.

Google admitted that their energy consumption increased by 37% from 2022 to 2023—surpassing their ability to scale renewable energy efforts. If left unchecked, the environmental cost of AI could escalate rapidly.

Electronic Waste Generation

Rapid technological advancements lead to frequent hardware upgrades, generating significant amounts of electronic waste. Improper disposal of e-waste can have serious environmental impacts.

In 2022, 62 million tonnes of e-waste was produced, but only 22.3% was documented as recycled. Toxic fumes, mercury poisoning, and resource over-extraction are all dangerous outcomes of poor e-waste handling.

Resource Intensive Manufacturing

The production of devices requires rare minerals and energy-intensive processes, which harm ecosystems. Toxins are released into air and water, while radioactive materials impact biodiversity.

To reduce harm, more sustainable materials and manufacturing practices must be adopted in the tech industry.

The Digital Divide and Uneven Benefits

Cutting-edge technologies can deepen inequality. Those without access to devices or reliable internet may fall behind in education, job opportunities, and access to services.

Planned obsolescence worsens this issue. As hardware requirements change rapidly, lower-income communities struggle to keep up, intensifying the digital divide.
